## Summary (AI-generated)

April content output stayed on schedule with **5 blog posts**, **2 newsletter sends**, and **1 gated resource** (the DFM Checklist for Ag-Equipment Engineers). The **5-axis CNC launch post** was the standout — **3,288 unique pageviews** in its first 19 days and a **6.1% CTR** from organic search, the highest of any blog post we've tracked for Prairie Ridge. Newsletter open rate held strong at **38.4%**, but click-through softened to **4.1%** (vs. 5.6% in March) — likely a reflection of the April 22 send leaning heavily on case-study content rather than capability-spec content. Recommend balancing the May editorial mix back toward 60% capability-anchored topics, since engineers are the dominant audience.

## Voice / brand consistency

The contextual memory layer for Prairie Ridge includes:
- Approved terminology (e.g., always "RFQ" not "quote request" in customer-facing copy; "fiber laser" not just "laser").
- Approved tone (engineer-to-engineer, technically credible, never marketing-speak).
- Approved spec language (capacity claims must include source — "350-ton press brake, AWS-certified weld cell").
- Forbidden claims (no lead-time promises in evergreen content; current quotes only).

April outputs (blog + newsletter + social): **100% on-brand checks passed** during Running Robots review. One spec-language correction made on Apr 9 post before publish (caught by Meegan during review).

## What we recommend for May

1. **Rebalance editorial mix** to 60% capability-anchored content (newsletter CTR softness signals engineers want capability/spec, not just stories).
2. **Refresh laser cutting capability content** — second strongest commercial intent cluster, currently ranking position 5–9 with room to climb.
3. **Add a second gated asset** — "Press Brake Tolerance Reference Guide" PDF, building on the DFM Checklist template.
4. **Test long-form video** on LinkedIn — short-form is plateauing; long-form (60–90s) capability walk-throughs are outperforming on competitor pages.
