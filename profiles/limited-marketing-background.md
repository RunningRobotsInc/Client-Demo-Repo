## Persona — Dale Henrickson, Owner & President, Prairie Ridge Manufacturing

I run Prairie Ridge. Took the company over from my dad in 2008 and have been the owner-operator ever since. I started on the shop floor at 17 — I know every machine in the building and most of our regular customers by first name.

My background is operations and finance. Purchasing, scheduling, quoting, hiring. **I have zero formal marketing training.** I hired Running Robots two years ago because my VP of Sales told me we were "invisible online compared to the competition." I trusted her recommendation and approved the engagement.

I read the monthly Running Robots report because she forwards it to me with notes. I usually have about **5 minutes** to spend on it. If something matters, I want to know in those 5 minutes.

## How I think about marketing

- It's a line item like any other. Is it making us money?
- I trust people who earn it. I've been pitched by three SEO companies in the last decade who delivered nothing — so my default is skeptical until shown otherwise.
- The metrics I actually care about are **RFQ count**, **close rate**, and **revenue from new customers vs. repeat**. Anything else feels like vanity until somebody connects it back to those three.
- I won't approve a budget change without understanding *why* in plain English.

## How to talk to me

### Use plain English. Translate every marketing term.

| Don't say | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "INP regressed by 44ms post-deploy." | "After the website update on April 2, the CNC machining page got noticeably slower to respond to clicks. Small in seconds, but enough that Google penalizes the page in search results." |
| "Sessions are up 18% MoM." | "About 2,200 more people visited the site in April than in March." |
| "RFQ conversion rate softened to 0.55%." | "About 1 out of every 181 visitors filled out our quote form, down from 1 out of 152 last month." |
| "Reallocate spend to the 5-axis CNC campaign." | "Move some of the ad budget from the campaign that isn't working onto the campaign that is. Specifically: take $1,200/month off the 'Tier-1 Fabricator' campaign and add it to the '5-Axis CNC Launch' campaign." |

### Lead with money and RFQs, not traffic.

Traffic numbers don't move me. RFQ numbers do. Always start your answer with what happened to **RFQs and revenue**, then explain the *why* underneath.

### One recommendation at a time. Spell out the consequence.

Don't give me five priorities. Give me the most important one and tell me what happens if we ignore it.

> ✅ "**Recommend:** Have our developer fix the slowdown on the CNC machining page in the next week.
> **If we don't:** Google will keep showing that page lower in search results, and we'll lose the traffic gains from the 5-axis launch — probably 10–15 fewer RFQs next month."

### Define every acronym the first time it appears, every report.

Even ones we use a lot. Assume I forgot.

- **CPL** — Cost Per Lead — what we paid in ads to get one RFQ form submission.
- **CTR** — Click-Through Rate — out of everyone who saw our ad, the percentage who clicked.
- **MoM / YoY** — Month-over-Month / Year-over-Year — comparing this month to last month, or this April to last April.

### Avoid this vocabulary entirely

bounce rate · dwell time · attribution · channel · funnel · top-of-funnel · segment · cohort · INP · LCP · CLS · conversion rate (use "RFQ rate") · engagement · sessions vs. users (just say "visitors") · organic vs. paid (say "from Google searches" / "from Google ads") · branded vs. non-branded · ICP · MQL · SQL · last-click · multi-touch

### End every answer with three lines

- **Bottom line:** [outcome in dollars or RFQs]
- **What we should do:** [single, specific action]
- **What it costs to ignore:** [consequence in dollars or RFQs]

## Worked example — how the same finding should be translated for me

**Question I asked:** "What happened to the website in April?"

### ❌ Bad answer (too technical)

> April sessions hit 14,082 (+18.4% MoM, +31.2% YoY) driven by organic on the new 5-axis CNC pages, but RFQ conversion rate softened to 0.55% from 0.66% — likely due to a 44ms INP regression on /capabilities/cnc-machining.

I'd read this and stop at "MoM." I'd skim to find a number I recognize, fail, and forward it to my VP. The whole point of the report is wasted.

### ✅ Good answer for me

> Big traffic month for the website — about 2,200 more people came to the site in April than in March, mostly from Google searching for 5-axis CNC machining. The new pages we launched in March are working.
>
> But here's what matters: the number of people who actually filled out a quote form stayed flat at 78. So we got more eyes on the site without getting more business out of it. The likely reason is that the website update we shipped on April 2 made the CNC machining page slower to click around. Google notices that and starts ranking the page lower in search results.
>
> - **Bottom line:** More eyes on the site, but we're leaking the new attention. RFQs flat instead of up.
> - **What we should do:** Have our developer fix the slowness on the CNC page in the next week.
> - **What it costs to ignore:** We lose the traffic gains from the 5-axis launch — probably 10–15 fewer RFQs next month, which translates to roughly 2–4 fewer closed deals over the next 90 days.
